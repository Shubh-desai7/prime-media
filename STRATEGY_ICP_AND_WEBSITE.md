# Prime Media — ICP, Problem, Website Role & "Eye Candy" (v2)

Scope: **Floor Projection** and **MagixPix Photobooth** only.
Legend: **[Client]** = told to us by the client or team. **[Source]** = from the client's old site files. **[Inferred]** = my hypothesis, to validate.

**What changed from v1:** the primary ICP is now wedding and social-event planners, venues and event-company owners. Brands, agencies and malls are secondary. Prime Media does **not** sell software or machines. It provides a **rented, set-up-and-run service**.

---

## 1. The promise

> **Make your event worth remembering, and give every guest something to take home.** **[Client]**

Everything on the site should serve this one line. It has two halves:

| Half | Meaning | Product |
|---|---|---|
| **Worth remembering** | A moment, an experience, a reason guests talk about the event afterwards | **Floor Projection** |
| **Take something home** | A tangible keepsake in each guest's hand | **MagixPix Photobooth** (printed copies and digital copies) |

## 2. The business model **[Client]**

| | Floor Projection | MagixPix Photobooth |
|---|---|---|
| **What the customer buys** | An experience for their event | An experience plus keepsake copies, printed and digital |
| **Delivery** | Prime Media brings, sets up and runs it for the booked time | Same |
| **Price driven by** | **Time rented** | **Time rented and number of copies** |
| **What they don't buy** | Software or equipment | Software or equipment |

Implications:
- It is a **service business**, so trust, punctuality and on-site crew matter more than specs. Technical details (Kinect, sensors, projectors) should be minimised on the site.
- Every booking is **date-specific**. The key question on the site is "Are you free on my date?"
- **No prices on the site for now [Client].** The site is lead-focused: every page ends in "Check your date" or "Get a quote". Pricing (time and copies) stays an internal conversation, so the site only says how it works in plain words if at all, with no numbers or estimator.
- A **permanent install** for malls and play zones is **an option the client can work out case by case [Client]**. It is not a headline offer, and the site mentions it only as "long-term setups on request" in the secondary section.

People who sell "a great event" to a family or a client, and want a guest attraction to differentiate it.

| Attribute | Profile |
|---|---|
| **Buyer** | **Event-company owner / wedding planner** (the decision maker), venue or banquet manager (a referral and recommendation channel) |

**Why they matter most:** one planner or venue can bring many bookings a year. The website should be built so planners can quickly check what's offered, see their kind of event, and forward a link to their client.

## 4. The problem, restructured

### The core problem
> Guests enjoy an event, but a few weeks later it blurs into every other event. Hosts and planners want to give guests **a moment they remember** and **something they can hold**, and most events offer neither.

### By who feels it
| Person | Pain | How Prime Media answers |
|---|---|---|
| **Planner / event-company owner** | "Every wedding looks the same. My client wants something different and I don't want to risk it on the day." | A distinctive, reliable guest attraction, set up and run by Prime Media's crew |
| **Hosting family** | "I want guests to enjoy it and remember it. Photos are scattered on phones and nobody prints them." | Floor Projection creates the memorable moment. MagixPix hands every guest a printed keepsake before they leave. |
| **Guest** | "Nice party. Where are the photos? I forget by next week." | A physical photo with the event's name and date, in the hand, that night |
| **Venue manager** | "I want to offer something extra that gets us recommended." | A bookable add-on that makes the venue stand out |
| **Brand/agency (secondary)** | "People pass our stall and forget us." | The same experience and keepsake, carrying the brand's frame |

### Why now **[Inferred]**
Phones fill with photos nobody revisits, and guests increasingly expect an "experience" at weddings and parties. A printed photo and a shared dance-floor moment are the opposite of another scrolling feed.

### One-line value proposition (draft)
*Prime Media brings a dance floor that comes alive and a photobooth that sends every guest home with a printed memory, so your event is the one people remember.*

## 5. How the website helps

### The job of the site
Get a planner or host to **check a date and request a quote** quickly, with confidence that Prime Media is reliable. The site is a booking tool, not a catalogue.

| What the site does | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Shows real events and real keepsakes.** Wedding and party videos, and photos of printed strips in guests' hands. | The product is emotional and visual. Seeing a dance floor reacting and a printed photo does the selling. |
| **Answers "Are you free on my date?"** A simple date-and-event form that goes straight to WhatsApp and email. | The decision is date-driven, and enquiries mostly come on WhatsApp **[Inferred]**. |
| **Makes asking easy.** No prices are shown **[Client]**. Instead the site says "Tell us your date and event and we'll send a tailored quote", with a reply promise such as "within a few hours" (client to confirm). | Turns price curiosity into a lead conversation. |
| **Builds trust for a live-event risk.** The headline proof is **"50+ weddings across Gujarat and Maharashtra" [Client]**, shown in the hero and trust strip, alongside past events, testimonials from planners and venues, "what happens on the day" (setup time, crew, backup). | Nobody wants an unfamiliar vendor failing at a wedding. |
| **Equips planners and venues.** A shareable link and a short PDF for their clients, and a "partner with us" section. | Planners and venues are the repeat-business channel. |
| **Gets found locally.** "Photobooth for wedding in Valsad", "interactive dance floor Gujarat", plus Google Business Profile. | Planners and families search close to the date. |
| **Looks alive and current.** The old site looks like 2011. | A modern site signals an active, reliable company. |

### Page set
1. **Home.** The promise, hero reel, two experience cards, proof, "Check your date".
2. **Floor Projection.** Wedding and party use, themes, video, how it works (we bring, set up and run it), FAQ (no prices).
3. **MagixPix Photobooth.** Guest flow, sample printed strips and designs, printed and digital copies, FAQ (no prices).
4. **Events / Gallery.** Filter by wedding, sangeet, birthday, corporate. Video and photos.
5. **Plan your event.** Date, event type, city, which experience (floor, photobooth or both), and optional guest count. Goes to WhatsApp and email. No price fields.
6. **For planners & venues** (can be a section at first). Partner info and a shareable pack.
7. **For brands & malls** (small secondary section or page).

### Metrics **[Proposed]**
- Date-check / quote requests per month (the main goal)
- WhatsApp click-throughs and calls
- Share of requests from planners and venues (repeat channel)
- Local search visits

## 6. "Eye candy" ideas, reframed around memory and keepsake

All use the brand colors (violet `#5002B0`, lime `#A3FA04`) and the lime beam from the logo.

### Tier 1: build first
1. **Hero reel of a real dance floor coming alive.** Loop of guests stepping on a glowing floor, the lime beam wiping across to reveal *"Make your event worth remembering."*
2. **Interactive floor-ripple.** On the Floor Projection page, the visitor's cursor or finger leaves glowing ripples on a dark floor. Offer **theme toggles** (petals, sparkles, diyas, garba-style patterns) so planners can feel the options. Confirm which themes actually exist before showing them.
3. **"Design your keepsake" photo-strip builder.** The visitor types a name, event and date (for example "Riya & Arjun · 14 Feb"), picks a frame color, and a polaroid strip updates live. It is the strongest "take something home" visual and also a lead capture: "Like it? Check your date."
4. **Keepsake formats showcase.** A tactile gallery of the printed options (paper types, wooden pieces, non-frame prints) that the visitor can tilt or flip on hover. It shows that the keepsake can be customised, with no prices.
5. **Photo-strip "print" animation.** A strip slides out of the booth in the hero or on scroll, with the lime beam as the light across the paper.

### Tier 2: strong differentiators
6. **Event-type picker.** Wedding, sangeet, birthday, corporate, kids. The page re-orders examples, photos and suggested hours for that occasion.
7. **"Build your event" mini-form.** Choose occasion, experience and approximate guest count, and a friendly summary is sent to the client as a lead. It shows no prices.
8. **Before and after slider.** The same banquet floor plain and then lit with the interactive effect.
9. **Guest-wall of real keepsakes.** A masonry grid of printed strips from real events, with permission, to signal that guests really take them home.
10. **Date checker.** A calendar where the client marks unavailable dates, so the visitor sees "Free" or "Booked" at once. Needs a way for the client to update it, such as a Google Calendar feed.

### Tier 3: polish
11. Dark mode on product pages so projection glow looks real.
12. Hover-to-play video tiles in the gallery.
13. Lime pulse on the main CTA, and a loading animation based on the P monogram.
14. Seasonal hero (wedding season, Navratri, Diwali), swappable by the client.

### Guardrails
- Mobile and WhatsApp first: most planners and families will open the site from a link on a phone. Target under 3 s on 4G.
- Compress video, lazy-load, provide a poster image, and respect reduced-motion settings.
- Use real photos with consent. Weddings are private, so get permission to show guests' faces.
- Keep contrast to the brand guide's approved pairings.
- Do not let any effect push the "Check your date" button off the first screen.

## 7. Decisions made and remaining questions

**Decided [Client]**
- Permanent installs for malls and play zones: an **option** the client figures out per enquiry, not a listed offer.
- MagixPix delivers **printed and digital copies**.
- The site is **lead-focused with no pricing details** for now.

- **Digital copies are delivered by email and WhatsApp only, for now [Client].** No social sharing, QR or gallery pages at launch.
- **Printed copies** come in **different paper types, wooden formats and non-frame formats, and can be customised [Client].** The site shows these as a visual "keepsake formats" gallery with no prices.
- **A point of contact (POC) handles all enquiries [Client].** The site routes every lead to the POC by WhatsApp and email. The POC's name, number and reply time are needed.
- **Proof headline: "50+ weddings across Gujarat and Maharashtra" [Client].** Coverage is Gujarat and Maharashtra.

**What "referral rate" means (for the client to decide):** the reward Prime Media gives a planner or venue who sends a booking, usually a **commission** (a percentage of the booking) or a **discount on their own client's booking or preferred rate**. It is not needed to launch. It only decides whether the "For planners & venues" section can say "earn on every referral", so it can be left out of the site for now and added once the client picks a model. Ask them: *do we currently pay or discount anyone who refers us?*

**Still open**
1. **Exact formats:** the names of the paper types and wooden or non-frame options, with a sample of each to photograph.
2. **Customisation:** what can be changed (names, date, theme, logo, layout) and how far in advance the design is needed.
3. **Floor Projection themes and effects** actually available for weddings and parties.
4. **POC details:** name, WhatsApp number, email, working hours, and a promised reply time.
5. **Proof:** wedding videos and printed-keepsake photos with guest consent, 3–5 planner or venue testimonials, and a list of cities and venues we may name.
6. **Evidence for "50+ weddings":** a rough list internally so the claim is safe to publish, and a decision on whether to say "50+" or a more exact figure.
7. **Hyderabad and Chennai:** still served, or past events only?
